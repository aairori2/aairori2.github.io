About

I'm a Nebraska-based soil data scientist and Soil Research Technologist at the University of Nebraska–Lincoln Institute of Agriculture and Natural Resources (IANR). I have done quantitative soil research since 2018, building soil carbon, greenhouse gas, and geospatial systems from field design to audit-ready reporting.

On a multi-state, USDA-funded project, I run soil carbon fractionation from field sampling through lab analysis and reporting, measure greenhouse gases with field chambers, and write the metadata and QA/QC that support USDA and partner audits. I model soil nitrogen with Gaussian process and Random Forest methods and Google Earth Engine data, and I check results on held-out data before trusting them. My work focuses on Midwest grazing, cropping, and grassland systems, specifically in Nebraska.

I hold an M.S. in Natural Resource Sciences and a B.S. in Environmental Science, both from UNL. I also worked as a Remote Research Consultant for the World Resources Institute, researching sustainable energy and agricultural policy in Southeast Asia.

I believe soil health management is where agriculture is heading. I share code and aggregated data on GitHub (the raw project data are protected), and I use AI tools to speed up coding and troubleshooting, checking their output against held-out data and a documented changelog.

Soil nitrogen forecasting

Asked whether five seasons (2019–2023) of field data from a grazing and cover-crop experiment (nine one-acre plots, 1,363 soil samples) can support a nitrogen forecast. I screened nine soil measurements for completeness, linked the data to Google Earth Engine inputs (Sentinel-1/2, Landsat, GRIDMET, a drought index), and fit Gaussian process and Random Forest models with KNN imputation. A held-out 2023 season showed the prediction intervals reached 100% coverage only because they were wider than the data, so five seasons support description, not prediction. I distilled the project into a five-check framework for forecasting from a short record.
